# Magnetic transition-metal (3d) ions

The 3d shell is the outer shell of the ion, so the crystal field quenches
the orbital moment: the moment is close to the spin-only value g·S with
g = 2 (CrysPy takes the Lande g of the table below when lande is 0). The valence
depends on the compound: in oxides, fluorides and chlorides it follows from
the charge balance with the anions (Fe in Fe2O3 is Fe3+, Mn in MnF2 is
Mn2+, Mn in LaMnO3 is Mn3+); in metals and intermetallics the moments are
itinerant and usually much smaller than the ionic values. The usual ion
below is the most common one in oxides.

Ordered moments are typically 10–20 % below g·S (covalency, zero-point
fluctuations); in low-dimensional and frustrated magnets (S = 1/2 chains
and planes of Cu2+) much more. Co2+ in an octahedron keeps a large orbital
moment (ordered moments up to about 4 μB). Low-spin ions in a strong
octahedral field: Co3+ (3d6, S = 0, nonmagnetic, as in LaCoO3 at low
temperature), Ni3+ (3d7, S = 1/2), Fe2+ in some cyanides (S = 0).
