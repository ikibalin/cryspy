# Usual charges of the non-magnetic ions

The valence of a magnetic ion in an ionic compound follows from the charge
balance: the charges of all ions of the unit cell (multiplicity × occupancy
× charge) add up to zero. CrysPy uses it to propose the magnetic ion of a
transition metal when the compound has O, F, Cl, Br, I, S or Se and all its
other elements are in the table below with a fixed charge. The balance
does not apply to metals and intermetallics (silicides, germanides,
borides, carbides: no ionic charges) nor to compounds with anion-anion
bonds: polysulfides (FeS2: S2 dimers, Fe2+), polytellurides (RTe3, the Te
layers are partly bonded) and peroxides; then the usual ion of the element
is proposed. A non-integer result means mixed valence (Fe3O4: Fe2+ and Fe3+;
La0.7Sr0.3MnO3: Mn3+ and Mn4+). Tellurium, arsenic, antimony and the
elements with several common charges (Sn, Pb, Bi, Tl) are left out.
