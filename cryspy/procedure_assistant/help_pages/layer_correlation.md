A layered crystal can be ordered inside its layers while the layers are
not correlated with each other: two-dimensional magnetic order above the
three-dimensional ordering temperature, stacking disorder of van der Waals
crystals. The scattering is then concentrated on rods (hk) normal to the
layers instead of Bragg points. In a powder the rods give asymmetric
saw-tooth lines (B. E. Warren, Phys. Rev. 59 (1941) 693): a sharp front at
the 2theta of the two-dimensional reflection (hk) and a long tail to high
angles, several degrees long. Microstrain or small domains give symmetric
broadening, and the asymmetry p1-p4 of the instrument acts only within a
few widths of every peak: neither of them gives such a line.

## The loop layer_correlation

In a `pd` experiment (1D, constant wavelength, neutrons) a phase gets
layers with the loop `layer_correlation`:

```
loop_
_layer_correlation_phase_label
_layer_correlation_normal_h
_layer_correlation_normal_k
_layer_correlation_normal_l
_layer_correlation_contribution
_layer_correlation_length
_layer_correlation_scale
  TbTe3  0 1 0  magnetic  85.(4)  0.83(2)
```

- `normal_h normal_k normal_l`: Miller indices of the layer planes; the
  normal of the layers is the reciprocal vector h a* + k b* + l c*.
- `contribution`: which order exists only inside the layers.
  `magnetic` (default): the magnetic Bragg intensity of the phase is
  taken away and calculated as magnetic rods from the ordered moments of
  the crystal (`atom_site_moment` in a magnetic space group; the moments
  stay refinable). `nuclear`: the nuclear reflections off the 00l line of
  the stacking become nuclear rods (the 00l reflections stay Bragg
  reflections, the layer spacing is kept). `nuclear and magnetic`: both.
- `length`: correlation length L in the plane of the layers, Å (Warren's
  mean layer dimension), refinable.
- `scale`: scale of the rods with the meaning of the scale of the phase,
  refinable. When the whole phase has two-dimensional order it is equal
  to the scale of the phase; a smaller ratio is the part of the sample
  with the two-dimensional order.

Without the loop (or for the other phases) nothing changes.

## The calculation

The atoms of the unit cell are generated by symmetry and cut into layers
at the widest empty gaps along the normal (equivalent gaps are all cut).
For every rod G (the projections of the reciprocal lattice on the plane
of the layers, the rod through the origin excluded) the intensity along
the rod is

    h(s_z) = d_N Σ_layers |F_layer(G + s_z n)|²

(d_N: distance between the planes; nuclear: scattering lengths; magnetic:
the part of the magnetic structure factor perpendicular to the scattering
vector, so the directions of the moments change the intensity along the
rod, and the magnetic form factor). The in-plane correlations are
Gaussian, p(Δs) = L exp(-π L² Δs²), and the powder average of a rod at
s = 2 sinθ/λ is

    D(s) = [h(+u) + h(-u)] J / s,   u = sqrt(s² - g²),
    J = sqrt(4 L / (sqrt(π) (s + g))) F(a),   a = sqrt(π) L (s - g),
    F(a) = ∫_0^∞ exp(-(x² - a)²) dx,

g = |G|. At s = g this is the formula of Warren; far in the tail it is
the exact 1/sqrt(s² - g²) of a sharp rod. The rods keep the integrated
intensity of the Bragg reflections they replace (the sum of |F|² of the
single layers is the mean of |F_cell|² along the rod), so the same scale
gives the same total intensity. The line is convolved with the
instrumental profile of the experiment (U, V, W, IG, X, Y and the
asymmetry p1-p4, which are not replaced and keep their meaning).

Limitations: neutrons only; 1D `pd` only; isotropic displacement
parameters B in the Debye-Waller factor; the moments must be commensurate
(a magnetic cell with `atom_site_moment`): for an incommensurate order
use its commensurate approximant; a ferromagnetic moment of the whole
layer (the rod through the origin) is not calculated.

## Correlation between parameters

A large L (above a few hundred Å) makes the tail short and the line looks
like a peak with the instrumental asymmetry: then fix p1-p4 from a
standard or from the nuclear peaks and refine only L and the scale. The
scale of the rods and the moments are fully correlated when both are
refined from the rods alone.

## Which planes are the layers

`suggest_layer_direction` looks at the crystal structure: for every
direction (h k l) up to the index 2 the cell is cut at the widest empty
gap, and the best direction has the longest shortest bond across the gap
(weak, van der Waals like bonds) compared with the shortest bond inside
the layers. For the magnetic atoms it gives the shortest distances inside
and between the layers: a ratio well above 1 means two-dimensional
exchange. For TbTe3 (Cmcm) it suggests the planes (0 1 0): two Te sheet
gaps per cell, Tb-Tb 4.28 Å inside and 8.86 Å between the layers. It is
geometry only: charges and orbitals are not calculated.

## Procedures

- `suggest_layer_direction(phase_label, max_index)`: the table of the
  best directions and the suggestion.
- `add_layer_correlation(length, normal, contribution, phase_label,
  experiment)`: add the row (normal empty: the suggested planes; the
  start scale is the scale of the phase), refinement flags on.
- `report_layer_correlation(experiment)`: L with its error, the scale and
  its ratio to the scale of the phase, the layers per cell, the fronts
  of the first rods in 2theta.

The recipe `layer_correlation` does these steps.
